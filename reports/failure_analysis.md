# Failure Analysis

> Generated by `scripts/run_failure_analysis.py` on the CoRe U-Net
> (`outputs/abl_unet_core`) over the synthetic_lg test split. Each prediction is
> scored by a combined quality measure (½·IoU + ½·clDice); the worst cases are
> categorised by interpretable, image-derived heuristics (marking width, local
> contrast/shadow, component density, foreground coverage).

## 1. Failure-mode distribution (worst 20 predictions)

| Failure mode | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **shadow / low-contrast** | 13 | 65 % |
| high-density (intersections, zebra) | 4 | 20 % |
| other | 3 | 15 % |

Panels for the worst cases: `reports/visualizations/failure_analysis/worst_*.png`
(image \| GT \| prediction \| TP/FP/FN map), filename-tagged by category.

## 2. Interpretation (evidence-based)

**Shadows / low contrast dominate failures (65 %).** Evidence: the worst-scoring
frames are those with a cast-shadow band or low global contrast (gray std < 28).
Mechanism: a marking under shadow has a paint-to-asphalt contrast far below the
training mode, so the encoder's low-level edge responses fall under threshold and
the marking is missed (false negatives concentrate inside the shadow). This is
the single most actionable failure mode.
*Mitigation:* stronger photometric augmentation (shadow simulation, CLAHE),
shadow-invariant colour spaces, or a contrast-normalisation stem.

**High-density / intersection scenes (20 %).** Evidence: frames with ≥6 connected
marking components (zebra + arrow + multiple lanes) — predictions merge adjacent
stripes or break arrows. Mechanism: at fixed receptive field, closely-spaced thin
structures alias; the decoder cannot separate them at the trained resolution.
*Mitigation:* higher input resolution, the connectivity (clDice) term (which
explicitly discourages merging/breaking), or instance-aware heads.

**"Other" (15 %).** Borderline cases near the score threshold; no single dominant
cause.

## 3. Cross-reference to the width study

The width-stratified result (`loss_function_analysis.md`) shows that **thin
markings (2 px)** are where mIoU most under-reports quality. Notably, thin-marking
frames did *not* dominate this worst-case list — because at the *training* width
the model handles thin lines well, and the failure driver shifts to **photometric
nuisance (shadow)**. The two analyses are complementary: width stresses the
*metric*, shadow stresses the *model*.

## 4. Recommended next experiments

1. Add shadow/CLAHE augmentation and re-measure the shadow-failure share.
2. Train at 384²/512² and re-measure the high-density-failure share.
3. Re-run failure analysis on the thin (width 0.5) set to quantify the
   width×shadow interaction.
