## 2. Reading the table (evidence, not opinion)

**Skip-connection encoder-decoders (U-Net, U-Net++) win for thin markings.**
Evidence: they top *both* region (mIoU 0.93-0.94) *and* boundary metrics
(BoundaryIoU 0.986-0.987). Mechanism: their full-resolution decoder with dense
skip connections preserves the high-frequency detail that *is* a 2-8 px marking.
This empirically confirms the structural hypothesis from `literature_review.md`
(U-Net was designed for thin membranes ≈ lane lines).

**PSPNet is decisively worst on boundaries (BoundaryIoU 0.62).** Evidence:
mIoU 0.63 and the lowest clDice/TolIoU. Mechanism: PSPNet's pyramid-pooling
context comes at the cost of a coarse output stride and no detail-recovery
decoder — exactly the property thin structures cannot tolerate. The literature
prediction (★★★☆☆ for road markings) is confirmed.

**SegFormer beats DeepLabV3+ on *boundary/topology* despite lower mIoU.**
Evidence: SegFormer BoundaryIoU 0.918 > DeepLab 0.898 and TolIoU 0.959 > 0.946,
yet mIoU 0.847 < 0.878. **This is a concrete metric-disagreement / mis-ranking
case**: ranking by mIoU puts DeepLab above SegFormer; ranking by the
boundary/topology metrics flips them. With only 3.7 M params (7× smaller),
SegFormer is also the efficiency winner — its global attention recovers marking
*continuity* well, while its lightweight MLP decoder slightly under-resolves exact
width (hence lower mIoU). A deployment decision driven by mIoU alone would pick
the wrong model here.

## 3. Final research question

> **"What architecture is best suited for UAV-based road-marking
> perception, and why?"**

**Empirical answer:** a **skip-connection encoder-decoder — U-Net++ (or U-Net) —
trained with the CoRe (CE+Dice+clDice) loss.**

Supporting evidence (all measured, this repo):
1. **Detail/boundary quality** (the dominant requirement for 2-8 px markings):
   U-Net++ has the best BoundaryIoU (0.987) and clDice (0.999); PSPNet, lacking a
   fine decoder, is worst (0.62).
2. **Region accuracy:** U-Net/U-Net++ lead mIoU (0.93-0.94).
3. **Loss:** CoRe ≥ CE+Dice on every metric and improves topology under width
   shift (`loss_function_analysis.md`); recall-only Tversky destroys topology.
4. **Robustness to width/altitude shift:** topology metrics stay ≥0.90 across 2-8
   px for these models (`cross_dataset_generalization.md`).

**Caveat (efficiency axis):** if **on-board real-time** inference is the binding
constraint (Jetson-class), **SegFormer-B0** is the recommended choice — 7× fewer
parameters, competitive boundary/topology quality, and the best accuracy/latency
trade-off; pair it with the C++/TensorRT deploy path (`deploy/`). Thus the answer
is **U-Net++ for maximum accuracy, SegFormer-B0 for deployment**, both with CoRe.

**Validity caveat:** these numbers are on the controlled synthetic benchmark.
The *mechanisms* (skip-connections help thin structures; coarse decoders hurt;
mIoU mis-ranks on boundaries) are architecture-intrinsic and expected to hold on
SkyScapes/the target UAV dataset; the protocol to confirm is in `final_dataset_selection.md`.

## 4. Mis-ranking summary (the paper's secondary claim)

| Ranking by… | 1st | 2nd | 3rd | 4th | 5th |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **mIoU** | U-Net | U-Net++ | DeepLabV3+ | SegFormer | PSPNet |
| **BoundaryIoU** | U-Net++ | U-Net | SegFormer | DeepLabV3+ | PSPNet |

DeepLabV3+ and SegFormer **swap** between the two rankings → mIoU alone is an
insufficient selection criterion for thin road markings. QED for the thin-structure
evaluation thesis.
